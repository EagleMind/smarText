How smarText adds up your notes

smarText is a free Markdown editor that reads the amounts in what you write and adds them up. There are no formulas and no cells: you write a recipe, a mix or a shopping list the way you would on paper.

What counts as an amount

A number with a unit, a currency or a percent sign, anywhere in the text: 46g, 2 cups, 45 minutes, $12.50, 20%. It can sit in a sentence, a list item or a table cell.

A bare number is not an amount. "3 eggs" and "in 2024" are left alone, so ordinary writing doesn't turn into sums by accident. Amounts in headings, code and links are left alone too.

What adds up together

  • Amounts on the same line add up, and so do the lines that follow, even across blank lines.
  • A heading, or a line with no amount in it, starts a new sum.
  • Each amount shows its share of the total as a small tag beside it.
  • The total appears under the last line of the sum.

Units that mix

Units of the same kind are converted and added together. "1 kg flour, 500 g butter and 20 g salt" comes to 1,520 g. "2 cups milk with 100 ml cream" comes to 2.42 cups. The total is shown in the unit you wrote most often, or the first one when it's a tie.

Full words work as well, such as "4 ounces" or "2 tablespoons". Each kind gets its own total, so one paragraph can end with "1,520 g, 2.61 cups, and 140 min in total". Cups, spoons, pints and gallons are US measures, and "cal" is the food calorie.

Scale a recipe by changing its total

Click a total, type the total you want and press Enter. Every amount in that sum is rewritten in proportion. Change "83 g in total" to 500 and the line becomes "277g cocoa powder and 223g cocoa butter", with the same shares as before.

  • Each amount keeps its own unit, so cups stay cups and milliliters stay milliliters.
  • Only that kind changes: a new weight leaves the baking time alone.
  • The whole change is one undo step.

What it's good for

  • Recipes and baker's percentages: doughs, chocolate, sauces, cocktails.
  • Formulas where proportions matter: soap, candles, resin, paint, plant feed.
  • Shopping lists and small budgets, per currency.
  • Workouts and schedules, where the minutes and hours add up.
  • Cutting lists and room sizes in metric or feet and inches.

Questions

Do I need to learn formulas or a special syntax?

No. Write a number with a unit anywhere in a sentence, a list or a table. smarText finds the amounts and adds them up as you type.

Can I mix units such as kilograms and grams, or cups and milliliters?

Yes. Units of the same kind are converted and added together: 1 kg and 500 g make 1.5 kg, and 2 cups and 100 ml make 2.42 cups. Cups, spoons, pints and gallons are US measures.

How do I scale a recipe up or down?

Click the total, type the total you want and press Enter. Every amount in that sum is rewritten in proportion, each in its own unit, and one undo puts them all back.

Does smarText change my text?

Not when it calculates. Shares and totals are drawn on top of the note and are not part of the Markdown. Your text changes only when you change a total yourself.

Where is my note stored?

In your browser, on your device. It is not sent to a server, and there is no account.

Does it convert currencies?

No. Each currency is added up on its own, so dollars and euros get separate totals.
